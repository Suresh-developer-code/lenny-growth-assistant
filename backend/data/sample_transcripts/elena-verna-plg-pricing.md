Elena's core argument: most PLG companies treat pricing as a finance decision made once a year,
when it should be treated as a growth experiment run continuously, the same way you'd test an
onboarding flow.

She broke down the three PLG monetization models she sees most: usage-based (you pay for what you
consume — Snowflake, Twilio), seat-based (you pay per user — Slack, Notion), and feature-gated
freemium (free tier is full-featured but capped, paid unlocks scale or advanced features — the
early Dropbox/Zoom model). Her strong opinion: usage-based pricing grows faster in the current
market because it removes the "who do I even count as a seat" negotiation that slows down initial
adoption, but it's harder to forecast revenue on, so it suits companies with high consumption
variance and hurts companies with flat, predictable usage.

On the freemium-to-paid conversion specifically, Elena said the single biggest lever isn't the
paywall itself, it's where you place the "aha moment" relative to the paywall. If a user hits the
value moment (the thing that makes them go "oh, I get it now") *before* they hit a usage limit,
conversion improves significantly, because they're paying to keep something they already love, not
paying to find out if they'll love it.

She also flagged a common mistake: companies raise prices to fix a broken funnel instead of fixing
the funnel. If your free-to-paid conversion is low because the product doesn't clearly deliver the
promised value, raising the price of the paid tier doesn't fix that — it just makes the mismatch
between price and perceived value worse.
