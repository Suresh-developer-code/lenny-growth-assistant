Casey opened by explaining why he moved away from thinking about growth as a funnel and started
thinking in loops instead. A funnel has a start and an end — you pour people in the top and count
how many come out the bottom. A loop, by contrast, feeds itself: the output of one user's action
becomes the input for acquiring the next user.

He walked through the classic Pinterest loop as an example: a user pins content, that pin gets
indexed and shows up in search results, a new user finds that pin through search, signs up to see
more, and then pins their own content — which restarts the loop. The key growth metric isn't "top
of funnel traffic," it's the loop's "amplification factor" — for every user who enters, how many
new users does their activity bring in.

Casey argued that most growth teams over-invest in acquisition channels and under-invest in
understanding which loop they're actually running. He listed four loop archetypes he sees most
often: content loops (SEO-driven, like Pinterest or Quora), viral loops (referral-driven, like
Dropbox's classic double-sided referral), paid loops (where LTV funds the next round of paid
acquisition, like early Uber), and sales loops (where expansion revenue funds more sales capacity,
common in B2B).

On diagnosing which loop you're in, Casey's practical tip: look at your cohort retention curve
alongside your channel mix. If retention is flat but paid CAC is rising, you likely don't have a
loop yet — you have a funnel with a leak, and no amount of top-of-funnel spend will fix that.
