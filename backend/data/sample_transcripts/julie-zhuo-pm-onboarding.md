Julie described the biggest onboarding mistake she sees at high-growth companies: treating the
first 30 days as a documentation-reading exercise instead of a trust-building exercise. New PMs
read wikis, sit in meetings, and still don't know who to go to when something breaks.

Her framework has three phases. Phase one, days 1-14, is "shadow and ship something tiny" — the
new PM pairs with an existing PM on their actual roadmap and ships one small, real change (a copy
tweak, a small experiment) so they experience the full cycle: spec, review, ship, measure. Phase
two, days 15-30, is "own one metric" — the new PM is handed a single, well-scoped metric they're
accountable for, small enough to move in a month. Phase three, day 31+, is normal ramp into full
scope.

On 1:1s specifically, Julie's rule: a new manager should have a 1:1 with a new report inside the
first 48 hours, not the first week, because the goal in week one is answering "who do I trust here"
before anything else.

She also talked about a mistake she made early in her own management career: giving new PMs too
much unstructured "figure it out" time, believing it built independence. In practice it just built
anxiety, because ambiguity without any early wins erodes confidence before it builds resilience.
Her fix was the "own one metric" phase — enough structure to guarantee an early win, but a real
metric, not a toy project.
